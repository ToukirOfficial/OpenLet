# OpenLet — PRD

**One line:** free, open-source property and tenancy management for small landlords and small letting agents: properties, tenants, rent, repairs, documents and certificates in one place, with a portal for tenants.

**Owner:** Toukir Ahmed Rony · **Licence:** AGPL-3.0 · **Repo:** public on GitHub · **Platform:** web only (responsive, mobile-first for tenants) · **Demo:** openlet.toukirahmedrony.com

The full, prioritised feature list is in `FEATURES.md`. This PRD explains the why, the users, the rules and what "done" means. Build every **[V1]** item in `FEATURES.md`; build **[V1+]** items only after all [V1] items pass their checks; never build **[Later]** items.

## 1. The problem

Most UK landlords own one to a few properties, and many small letting agents manage under 50. They run everything on spreadsheets, WhatsApp, email and paper: who has paid rent, which repair is still open, when the gas safety certificate runs out, where the tenancy agreement is. Things get missed: late rent isn't chased, repairs drag on, certificates expire, deposits aren't protected in time. Full property management systems cost a monthly fee per property and are built for bigger agencies. OpenLet is a free, self-hosted system that a small landlord or agent can run on their own server and use from day one.

## 2. How it is used

OpenLet is **self-hosted open-source software, not a SaaS**. A landlord or a letting agency (or a developer working for them) installs it on their own server with `docker compose up`. On first visit, a setup screen creates their Owner account and workspace. It is then their own private system: their team, their tenants and their data, on their server, for free. Open sign-up is off by default; `SIGNUP_ENABLED=true` allows one install to serve many businesses, so the code is ready for a hosted version later. The public demo uses fake data only and is reset nightly.

## 3. Users

- **Landlord or agency Owner:** sets up the workspace, team and settings; sees everything.
- **Manager / Staff:** day-to-day work: tenancies, rent, repairs, documents, messages.
- **Tenant:** a member of the public, mostly on a phone: checks rent, reports repairs, reads documents, messages the landlord.
- **Contractor:** a tradesperson who receives one job by link, updates it from their phone, no account.
- **Self-hoster:** a developer installing OpenLet for a landlord or agency.

## 4. Core user journeys (must all work end to end)

1. **Set up:** first-run setup creates the Owner and workspace → add a property → add a tenant → create a tenancy with rent and deposit → invite the tenant.
2. **Rent month:** rent charges appear automatically → reminder email goes before the due date → staff record a payment → balance updates → a late tenant appears in arrears and gets an overdue reminder → the tenant sees the same ledger in the portal.
3. **Repair:** tenant reports "boiler not working" with a photo → staff set priority "urgent" and assign a contractor → contractor opens the link, books a visit, uploads a photo, marks completed with a cost → tenant is emailed at each step → staff close it → cost appears in the maintenance report.
4. **Compliance:** staff upload a gas safety certificate with an expiry date → compliance board shows it valid → 60/30/7-day reminders fire before expiry → board turns amber then red if not renewed.
5. **End of tenancy:** notice recorded → tenancy shows "ending" → deposit return recorded with deductions → tenancy ends → after the retention period, tenant personal data is anonymised.

## 5. Business rules

- **Money:** integer pence, GBP. Never floats. Ledger entries are append-only: corrections are new entries (reversal + new entry), never edits. Balance = charges − payments − credits.
- **Rent charges:** generated by a scheduled job from the tenancy's frequency, due day and rent history, idempotent (a charge for a period is never created twice), never generated after the tenancy end date. Months without the due day (e.g. 31st) use the last day of the month. Weekly and four-weekly count from the tenancy start date.
- **Payment allocation:** to the oldest unpaid charges first; overpayment becomes a credit on the balance.
- **Dates:** stored in UTC, shown and calculated in Europe/London. "Due today" and "overdue" use the London calendar day.
- **Reminders:** each reminder (rent due, overdue step, certificate step, deposit, Right to Rent) is sent at most once per item and step, and stops when the reason is gone (paid, renewed, protected).
- **Tenant access:** a tenant only ever sees their own tenancies, ledger, shared documents, repairs and messages. Internal notes and internal repair comments are never visible to tenants.
- **Contractor links:** random, stored only as a hash, scoped to one repair, expire when the repair closes (plus a configurable grace period), revocable.
- **Compliance board statuses:** valid (more than 60 days left), expiring (60 days or less), expired, missing (required for that property type and not uploaded). Which document types are "required" is a workspace setting with sensible defaults (gas safety only if the property has gas).
- **Legal note:** the app shows that reminders are an aid, not legal advice. Never claim a property is "legally compliant".

## 6. Non-functional

- Mobile-first tenant portal and contractor page; staff app fully responsive.
- Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA.
- UK English; UK address and postcode formats; dates as "2 November 2026".
- Fast: dashboard under 1 second with the demo data; heavy work (emails, PDFs, image conversion, reminders, rent charge generation, exports) on queues.
- Privacy: no real personal data in seeds, tests, logs or screenshots; photo metadata stripped; documents private.

## 7. Design and landing page

This is a portfolio project: it must look like a polished commercial product within 30 seconds of opening it.

- **Brand:** OpenLet wordmark (simple SVG), a warm, trustworthy palette (one confident accent colour, deep neutral text, soft backgrounds, clear status colours for paid/overdue/valid/expiring/expired), a clean free open-licence font, consistent spacing, defined once as Tailwind theme tokens.
- **Dashboard:** stat cards, "needs attention" list, activity feed, calendar; status badges and progress indicators everywhere.
- **Property and tenancy pages:** clear summary header, tabs (overview, rent, repairs, documents, messages, timeline).
- **Tenant portal:** friendly, simple, big tap targets; "Report a repair" one tap from home.
- **Contractor page:** one screen, works on any phone, no login.
- **Empty states** that explain the next step; loading skeletons, not spinners; helpful error messages.
- **Landing page at /:** headline (e.g. "Run your rentals without the spreadsheets"), one-line explanation, product screenshots or an interactive tour, the 5 core journeys as short sections, who it's for, "free and open source, self-host in one command", buttons "Try the live demo" and "View on GitHub", FAQ.

## 8. Demo mode

`DEMO_MODE=true`: seeded realistic fake portfolio (about 8 properties in West Yorkshire with units, joint tenancies, 6+ months of rent history including some arrears, repairs in every status, certificates valid/expiring/expired/missing, contractors, messages), one-click "Try as landlord" and "Try as tenant", contractor demo link shown on a repair, all emails captured in a demo mail viewer (none sent), uploads limited to 2 MB and purged hourly, sign-up disabled, nightly reset.

## 9. Self-hosting

`docker compose up` runs web, API, worker, PostgreSQL and Redis. `.env.example` documents every setting. README covers install, first login, email setup, backups and upgrades.

## 10. README must include

Screenshots or a GIF, what it does, who it's for, feature overview, try the demo, self-host with Docker, architecture, hard problems and decisions (e.g. the rent ledger and charge generation, tenant isolation, contractor links), tests. Plain, simple English, same standard as the Slotwise repo.

## 11. Out of scope (version 1)

Payment processing, bank feeds, accounting (beyond reports and CSV), applicant tracking and referencing, public property listings, landlord-owner portal, native mobile apps, e-signatures, AI features, multi-currency.

## 12. Done means

- All 5 core journeys in section 4 work end to end in the browser, on desktop and phone.
- Tests pass for: login and refresh, every role and workspace-isolation rule, tenant isolation (a tenant can never read another tenancy), contractor link scope and expiry, rent charge generation (monthly incl. 29–31st, weekly, four-weekly, rent changes, end dates, idempotency), payment allocation and balances, reminder scheduling (each sent once, stops when resolved), compliance status calculation, data retention anonymisation.
- Demo live with one-click landlord and tenant login; landing page done; `docker compose up` works from a clean machine; README complete.
